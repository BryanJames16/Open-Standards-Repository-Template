# Licensing Standards Text

This guide concerns the **copyright license for the text of your standards and specifications**. It does not select licenses for software, tools, libraries, or other implementations of a standard.

## Keep the Licenses Separate

- The repository's root [`LICENSE`](../../LICENSE) applies to the template material within the scope stated by that license.
- [`SPECIFICATION-LICENSE.md`](../../SPECIFICATION-LICENSE.md) states the license chosen by your project for its standards text.

Make the scope of each license clear so contributors and readers know which terms apply to which material.

## Choose a License

Choose terms that match how you want people to use and share your standards text. For a permissive, commercially usable document license, **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)** is a reasonable starting point. It allows sharing and adaptation, including commercial use, provided users give appropriate credit, link to the license, and indicate changes.

You may choose another license if it better fits your project's requirements. Do not add a non-commercial or no-derivatives restriction unless that restriction is intentional.

- [CC BY 4.0 – License deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
- [CC BY 4.0 – Legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode)

## What to Do

1. Select the license for your standards text and state it in `SPECIFICATION-LICENSE.md`.
2. Make the license easy to find from the README and from each published specification, such as in its title page or document metadata.
3. Only include third-party text when its license permits your intended use, and retain any required attribution.

Use the same license consistently across project specifications unless there is a clear reason to state otherwise. If you are unsure whether a license fits your organization's requirements or the material you plan to publish, obtain appropriate legal advice before publication.

## Outside This Guide

This guide does not establish patent rights, trademark permissions, contributor agreements, or licensing for software and other implementations. Address those matters separately only if your standards project requires them.
