# Review Process

Reviews help ensure that contributions are clear, relevant, and technically sound. This guide describes repository reviews; formal decision-making and approval authority remain defined in [`GOVERNANCE.md`](../../GOVERNANCE.md).

## What Reviewers Check

Keep reviews proportionate to the change. Consider whether it:

- Fits the project's scope and relates to the stated proposal or issue.
- Is technically accurate, clear, and complete.
- Uses terms consistently and does not conflict with other parts of the specification.
- Explains significant changes and includes reliable references where needed.
- Distinguishes editorial corrections from changes that affect meaning or requirements.

Not every change needs a lengthy review. A typo correction may need only a quick check; a change to normative requirements may need broader technical review.

## Giving Feedback

Reviewers should identify the relevant text, explain the concern, and state whether a change is required or suggested. Authors should respond to comments and update the contribution or explain why they propose a different approach.

When a review comment is resolved, mark it as resolved in the pull request. Keep important unresolved concerns visible until they are addressed or handled through the project's decision process.

## Completing a Review

- **Reviewer:** Provides feedback and records whether any blocking issues remain.
- **Author or contributor:** Addresses feedback or explains the proposed resolution.
- **Maintainer:** Checks that repository requirements and required reviews are satisfied before merging.
- **Governing body or authorized decision-maker:** Makes any formal decision required by [`GOVERNANCE.md`](../../GOVERNANCE.md).

A GitHub approval or merged pull request is not formal approval of a standard unless [`GOVERNANCE.md`](../../GOVERNANCE.md) explicitly defines it as such.

## Public Review (Optional)

For a formal public review, follow the project's [standards lifecycle](../governance/standards-lifecycle.md). State where feedback should be submitted and how substantive comments will be considered. Record significant comment dispositions in the existing issue, discussion, or review record rather than creating duplicate paperwork.
